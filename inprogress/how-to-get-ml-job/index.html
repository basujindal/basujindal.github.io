Introduction#

I recently spent 7 months on the job market trying to get an ML job as a fresher. I hold a master’s and bachelor’s degree. After interviewing for 20 different roles with companies like Adobe, Snowflake, Zoom, AMD, Qualcomm, etc (I am choosing to omit the names of a few other companies and start-ups), I stopped applying upon receiving the first offer, which was, fortunately, from one of my target companies. This blog is biased toward the US market since I am currently based in the States.

During the past two years, I have seen many people, including my friends and myself, struggling to get a job in AI/ML, which motivated me to write this blog and share my experience. I am definitely not an expert, but my experience might help someone in the same boat.. Please correct me if I am wrong on any of the points.

What is AI/ML?#

When talking about jobs in AI and ML, I am talking about working on something related to large language models, diffusion-based models, Autonomous driving, Robotics, and similar domains. I am not referring to jobs in data science or analytics. Although AI and ML are not the same, I will use AI and ML interchangeably or simply AI/ML.

The Misconception#

The issue with jobs in AI/ML is the hype and the “seemingly” low barrier of entry. It is very easy to complete the Andrew NG specialization on Coursera, where he tells you not to worry even if you don’t understand the mathematics behind ML 1. After a few lectures, he says “Now you know more than 90% of the Silicon Valley engineers”. I am not criticizing Andrew NG, he is without any doubt, one of the best teachers and researchers in the field. And an IMO silver medal holder. But what people don’t understand when he refers to 90% of the Silicon Valley engineers is that he is referring to highly skilled people in domains like software, data, infrastructure, hardware engineering, etc., who want to learn the basics of ML.

Also, libraries like huggingface transformers make it trivial to download, run, and even fine-tune any ML model. Script kiddies trying to break into the field after learning these tools will be in for a rude shock when they start interviewing for jobs. They are good for people who specialize in the above-mentioned fields and just want to run/test and gain a basic understanding of the models or experts who want to prototype their ideas quickly. Running these scripts, and completing online courses takes little to no effort while giving a false sense of being interview-ready. Real effort is required when you start to understand the mathematics behind the models, the engineering skills required to serve these models, the data engineering skills required to collect and clean the data for these models, and the research to create new models.

What skills are required?#

I hear many people wanting to become “ML engineers.” When asked what exactly they want to work on, they say, they want to “train” an ML model, and by training, most of them refer to watching the loss curve go down. This is like saying I want to compete in F1 without knowing how to drive. [give a better analogy] Training a model is just the tip of the iceberg. The task of training an ML model requires a combined effort of skilled engineers working on various aspects of training and serving. If you want to work in AI/ML, ask yourself these questions:

  • Do you hold a PhD with papers in top conferences like NeurIPS, ICML, CVPR, ACL, EMNLP, etc.?
  • Have you optimized and served models in production?
  • Do you have a strong background in data engineering, such as ETL, data warehousing, data cleaning, etc.? Do you have a strong background in infrastructure engineering, such as managing large clusters, load balancing, autoscaling, etc.?
  • Have you trained models, including deciding the architecture, hyperparameters, loss functions, etc?
  • Do you have a strong background in hardware engineering, such as designing ASICs, FPGAs, etc.?

Why would a company hire a Bachelors/Masters’s graduate without any experience in the above? That is why a majority of ML roles require at least 3-5 years of experience. Also if you closely look, most of the above skills are general skills and not exclusive to ML. But then, how does a fresher break into the field? It is a catch-22 situation.

How to break into the field?#

Although AI/ML jobs usually require the above general skills, there are a few skills that are exclusive to AI/ML and are required when working with models like LLM and Diffusion. Most senior engineers either don’t have the time or the inclination to learn them, and people trying to break into the field can take advantage of them. Companies looking to expand their teams or build their AI/ML teams are looking for people who have the following skills.

  • Have a deep understanding of the mathematics behind the models like backpropagation, optimization algorithms, loss functions, activation functions, etc
  • Can solve at least the Blind 75 with ease
  • Open-source contributions with a good number of stars
  • First/Second-author publications as a Bachelor/Masters student in top conferences (third-author publication or a no-name conference won’t help and just add noise to the already overcrowded field)

For the above-mentioned points, below is a list of questions that I was asked in the interviews. First is a list of general questions, irrespective of the role and the content on your resume.

Interview Questions#

Leetcode#

  • Backtracking
  • detect cycle in a directed graph
  • Find subarray with maximum sum (can contain fraction)
  • 2 sum
  • Implement BFS, BFS, weighted traversal
  • How to create a dictionary from a list of words (Trie)
  • Topological sort
  • Find if an event can be scheduled with given events

ML coding#

  • Python use decorator
  • Implement a Decoder model
  • Implement Batch Normalization
  • Implement 2-D convolution in Numpy with padding and stride
  • Implement back-propagation in 2-layer NN

Traditional ML#

  • What is the difference between L1 and L2 regularization?
  • What is the difference between RNN, LSTMs and Transformers?
  • How do you handle class imbalance?
  • How do you handle missing data?
  • How to handle overfitting/underfitting?

Deep Learning#

  • Layer Norm, Batch Norm, RMS Norm, etc., and when should you use one over the other?
  • How do you handle vanishing/exploding gradients?
  • Explain the Attention mechanism and the intuition behind it.
  • Explain activations like ReLU, Swish, GLU, GELU, etc.

ML system design#

  • How do you serve a model in production?
  • Explain KV cache and the memory requirements for using it.
  • Why is Flash attention needed, and how does it work?

Diffusion#

  • How does a Diffusion model work? (Can explain using score-based modeling, Flow-based modeling, etc)
  • How is it different from GANs, VAEs, etc?
  • How is it different from autoregressive models like LSTMs, Transformers, etc?
  • Explain sampling techniques like DDPM, DDIM, etc

Large Language models#

  • Code decoder only Transformer
  • Explain Attention and the intuition behind it
  • Explain various types of position embeddings like RoPe, Absolute, etc.
  • Why is product of the Q and K matrix normalized

Quantization:#

  • Explain Quantization
  • Explain or implement AbsMax, Zero-point
  • Explain AWQ, Smooth Quant, and their drawbacks
  • Quant training techniques like QAT, PTQ, etc.

Misc#

Some interviews gave me a research paper on the spot, and I had to read and explain it in less than 20 minutes. There are two things to keep in mind for this. First, we assume that the paper is related to a field you deeply understand. Next, read the essential parts of the paper in less than 20 minutes. The general advice is to read the abstract, methodology, results, and conclusion. If you have time, read the introduction and the related work. {add link to some tweet of researcher}

General Advice#

Work Hard. Don’t just sit and watch YouTube tutorials or fine-tune Neural Networks using HF. Do tasks that require active effort like leetcode, doing good research, implementing new algorithms from scratch, making positive contributions to the Open-Source community unlike this.Also, don’t use tools like GitHub CoPilot, they make you lazy. Writing a working code from scratch takes effort, CoPilot is okay to use during job. There is also the case of a bad job market. Tech jobs, in general, have been decreasing after 2022 [add graph]. Andrew NG’s course might have helped 5 years ago when AI/ML field was not overly saturated.

Miscellaneous#

  • Use LinkedIn wisely. Most of the interviews I got were through cold messaging a hiring manager who posted a job on LinkedIn.
  • A referral definitely helps, but don’t wait for the other person to submit one for you so long that the job ID closes.
  • Keep learning: The AI/ML job market and the skills required are very dynamic. Keep yourself updated with the latest advancements and research. I have found that following high-signal accounts on Twitter is the way to keep up to date.
  • Play to your strengths: Don’t run after the “converted” ML job title, especially in this market. If you are good in a domain other than ML, let’s say hardware, and want to switch to ML, it might be better to postpone while working in your field of expertise.
  • Luck plays a big part. You might ace every interview, and they might still reject you.
  • Finally, keep your family close and your friends closer.
